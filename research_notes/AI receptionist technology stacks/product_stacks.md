# AI Phone Receptionist Products (Home Services and Restaurants): Engineering Stacks and Approach, 2024-2026

Research date: 2026-10-13. Method note: page fetches (WebFetch and curl) were blocked in this environment for almost every company domain (DNS failure or proxy 403). Every finding below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Treat exact wording as close paraphrase. "Confirmed" means a primary source (the company, the vendor it names, or a press release) states it. "Vendor claim" means the company says it about itself and nobody has checked it independently. "Inference" is my reasoning and is kept in the Inferences subsections.

---

## 1. Build vs buy: what platforms and components (STT, LLM, TTS, telephony) each product uses

### Takeaway
Very few of these products publicly name their voice stack. The only confirmed component disclosures I found: Yelp Host/Hatch (OpenAI GPT-Live-1 as the front voice layer on top of a "proprietary multi-provider" stack), ConverseNow (Rime TTS, plus a "custom system" that uses several LLMs), and SoundHound (its own in-house Polaris ASR/foundation model). No product on the list showed up as a named customer of Vapi, Retell, Bland, Synthflow, LiveKit, Pipecat, Deepgram, ElevenLabs or Cartesia in any case study I could find. The funded vertical leaders (Avoca, Netic, Hostie, Slang, Loman, Kea) all describe their tech as "proprietary" or trained on their own call data, and none name their vendors.

### Inferences
- A pattern runs across the field. The well-funded vertical players present a proprietary or "trained on N million calls" story and keep vendors private. This is probably a competitive and fundraising choice. It does not prove they built STT and TTS themselves. Most likely they assemble third-party STT, LLM and TTS behind their own orchestration and integration layer. ConverseNow, which licenses TTS from Rime and runs several LLMs while marketing a proprietary platform, is the clearest documented example.
- Yelp's design is the most explicit public model: a speech-to-speech model handles only the conversation, while deterministic business logic (service area, calendar, routing) sits underneath. This "voice front end over business logic" split is a sensible reference architecture.
- The SMB self-serve tools (My AI Front Desk, Dialzara, Rosie, Upfirst) are cheap ($29-$99/month tiers) and offer many languages and voice choices. That fits building on hosted voice-agent platforms or commodity STT/LLM/TTS APIs rather than a proprietary stack. This is inference only; no source confirmed it.
- Goodcall's Google speech pedigree, Twilio SIGNAL appearances and Searchlight award suggest Twilio telephony and possibly Google speech. This is inference and unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Not found, for any product: a vendor case study (Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Cartesia, Retell, Vapi, LiveKit, Twilio) that names Avoca, Sameday, Rosie, Goodcall, Dialzara, My AI Front Desk, Upfirst, Loman, Smith.ai, Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Netic, Slang, Hostie, Maple or Kea.
- Subprocessor lists for these products could not be read, because their sites were unreachable from this environment. Slang's policy extract named only Stripe. A manual check of each footer "subprocessors" or DPA page is the best next step.
- Siro (field sales call coaching) was not researched in depth. It records in-person sales conversations rather than answering phones, so it is likely out of scope as a receptionist.
- No information was found on My AI Front Desk's, Dialzara's, Rosie's or Upfirst's models, voices or telephony.

## 2. How they handle booking: integrations, tool calling, transfer, SMS

### Takeaway
The winners integrate deeply with the system of record. In trades that means ServiceTitan (capacity, job types, zones), Jobber and Housecall Pro. In restaurants it means the POS (Toast, Square, Clover, SpotOn, Shift4, NCR, Olo) and reservation books (OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms, Yelp). The usual fallbacks when the AI can't finish a task are warm transfer and SMS links. Several products, Slang most clearly, text a link instead of completing orders by voice.

### Inferences
- In trades, the moat is writing into the booking engine (ServiceTitan capacity, job types, zones). Simply collecting a message is not enough. ServiceTitan claims exclusive access to adaptive capacity, which implies third parties like Avoca and Sameday work through the certified app API with less capacity logic.
- Low-cost SMB tools gate real booking behind higher tiers and fall back to SMS links. This keeps the AI from making hard commitments.

### Gaps
- No product publicly documents its tool-calling design (function schemas, confirmation read-backs, retry or idempotency for bookings).
- I found no public data on double-booking or "hallucinated booking" rates for any product.

## 3. How they prevent wrong answers: scripted vs free LLM, knowledge bases, human review, hybrids

### Takeaway
The common pattern is constrained LLM plus human fallback. The agent is grounded in a per-business knowledge base or live integration data, routes anything ambiguous to a person, and is backed by human staff, either as live overflow (Smith.ai, Avoca's human-in-the-loop CSRs, Kea historically) or as reviewers who correct AI work (Avoca). Kea's account of the long road from 98% to 99.3% order accuracy shows how much engineering accuracy takes.

### Cited Findings
- **Smith.ai** sells three tiers: AI-only, Hybrid (AI first, with customer-chosen handoffs to North America-based human agents) and human-first. The caller stays on the line and the human gets the context the AI has collected. Smith.ai cites 75% of AI-first calls fully resolved by AI, and sets a defined AI/human split at onboarding. — [Smith.ai hybrid page](https://smith.ai/hybrid-ai-human-receptionists); [Lawyerist](https://lawyerist.com/news/choosing-between-ai-and-a-live-receptionist-smith-ai/)
- **Avoca.** Its FAQ claims 80%+ of inbound calls are handled with no human, with edge cases transferred. A "Human-in-the-Loop program of trained CSRs" stands by for after-hours coverage. Tyson Chen (2025 podcast) says some things "a human is just fundamentally better at," and describes warm transfers with context. A third-party case study says staff review and correct the AI's work. — [Owned and Operated #131](https://www.ownedandoperated.com/post/owned-and-operated-revolutionizing-call-centers-with-avoca-ai-and-tyson-chen); [The DataStory Substack](https://thedatastory.substack.com/p/case-study-avoca-and-voice-to-data); [Avoca site](https://www.avoca.ai/)
- A customer quote on Avoca's site says Avoca handles "70% of our entire call volume." The podcast headline says "100% of the time." Read that as answering every call, not resolving every call. — [Avoca site](https://www.avoca.ai/); [YouTube: Rilla Labs Ep. 15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Ls2ssHGJvQ)
- **Slang.ai.** Uses prompts built from more than 17M guest calls. When a request is "ambiguous or falls outside what the system is confident handling, it is designed to route the call to a person rather than risk a mistake." It forwards to staff or texts contact info when it can't answer. — [Slang blog](https://www.slang.ai/post/ai-phone-answering-system-restaurants); [Slang product](https://www.slang.ai/product)
- **ServiceTitan.** Voice agents are trained from a Knowledge Base for common questions. Booking is bounded by capacity, job types and zones. — [ServiceTitan KB release note](https://help.servicetitan.com/release-hub/docs/train-voice-agents-with-the-knowledge-base-to-answer-common-questions)
- **Housecall Pro CSR AI.** Owners refine scripts and tone. It discloses that it is an AI at the start of each call, and connects to the owner or a Housecall Pro representative when asked. — [Morgan Systems review](https://morgansystems.org/housecall-pro-csr-ai-review/)
- **Kea.** Started human-in-the-loop, launching "Indecisive AI" to switch to human operators when orders were unclear. Restaurant Business (2026) reports that "with human backup out of the picture," it took two and a half years of engineering to reach 99.3% accuracy, up from 98%. Loman, a competitor, says Kea's live reps still monitor calls. That conflicts with Restaurant Business. — [PMQ Pizza](https://www.pmq.com/indecisive-ai/); [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/how-one-voice-ai-provider-working-stand-out-crowded-market); [Loman vs Kea](https://loman.ai/blog/loman-vs-kea-orders-reservations)
- **ConverseNow.** Uses several LLMs, each applied where it is strongest. Reported a 7-8% accuracy gain after moving to LLMs. — [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/how-new-generation-chatbots-changing-voice-ordering)
- **Yelp.** Business logic (service area, calendar, routing) is kept outside the speech model. Both products are pre-trained on Yelp business data. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-and-hatch-put-openais-gpt-live-1-into-voice-ai-after-1-million-calls/)
- **Netic.** Its "Agent Platform" team builds tools that let agents "build, test, and improve themselves," which points to automated evaluation and iteration loops. — [Netic Ashby posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/netic/d9bcb6a2-0e54-4cb3-baec-43f2d74db18f)
- **Craft benchmark (vendor-published, Feb 2026).** Craft compared Craft, Avoca, Broccoli and Netic across 10,000 real HVAC, plumbing and electrical calls. It transcribed every call with AssemblyAI Universal 3 Pro and scored them with the same LLM-as-a-judge prompts. Metrics were lead qualification, booking rate and caller engagement. This shows the QA method in use, but Craft is a competitor ranking itself. — [Craft benchmark](https://www.craftflow.com/insights/ai-voice-agent-benchmark)

### Inferences
- The products with the largest deployments (Avoca, Smith.ai, Kea historically, ConverseNow at Domino's scale) all keep a human or a deterministic fallback path. None runs an unconstrained LLM.
- Automated call grading (transcription plus LLM-as-judge) appears to be standard QA practice in the category.

### Gaps
- No product publishes hallucination or error-rate metrics. Kea's 99.3% order accuracy is the only specific accuracy figure, and it is self-reported via press.
- No details found on prompt design, guardrail models or confirmation read-back policies for any product.

## 5. Compliance: SOC 2, HIPAA, recording disclosure, data training

### Takeaway
Compliance disclosures are sparse. Slang has SOC 2 (Type 1 at the time of its announcement). HIPAA claims at the SMB tier are inconsistent: Dialzara claims it, Rosie is disputed, Upfirst offers a BAA only on Custom. The biggest documented risk is legal. ConverseNow faces California wiretap (CIPA) and Illinois biometric (BIPA) class actions over Domino's calls, which shows that recording, disclosure and third-party "interception" exposure is real.

### Cited Findings
- **Slang.ai** announced SOC 2 compliance, describing it as SOC 2 Type 1. — [Slang blog](https://www.slang.ai/post/slang-ai-soc-2-compliance)
- Slang's terms allow it to use "Aggregated De-Identified Data" for its own business purposes. — [Slang Terms](https://www.slang.ai/terms-of-service)
- **ConverseNow.** A federal court let a California Invasion of Privacy Act class action proceed. It held that ConverseNow was an "intercepting third party," not covered by Domino's party exemption. A separate proposed Illinois BIPA class action alleges voiceprints were collected without consent. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/extra-large-pii-zza-courts-allows-california-privacy-class-action-proceed-use-ai); [Kiosk Industry](https://kioskindustry.org/voice-ai-liability-dominos-sued-over-ai-voiceprints/)
- **Housecall Pro CSR AI** discloses that it is AI at the start of every call. — [Morgan Systems](https://morgansystems.org/housecall-pro-csr-ai-review/)
- **Dialzara** claims HIPAA compliance on its $29 plan, a self-claim. Rosie's HIPAA status is disputed across comparison sites. Upfirst offers a BAA only on the Custom tier. — [Dialzara vs Rosie](https://dialzara.com/compare/dialzara-vs-rosie); [Keragon](https://www.keragon.com/blog/ai-receptionist-med-spa); [Upfirst blog](https://upfirst.ai/blog/rosie-alternatives)
- Among platforms, Retell is described as offering SOC 2 on all plans. This is third-party commentary and is relevant only if a product builds on Retell. — [Callmissed comparison](https://www.callmissed.com/en/blog/bland-ai-vs-synthflow-retell-vapi-best)

### Inferences
- The ConverseNow CIPA ruling means any third-party voice-AI vendor recording California calls should get explicit up-front disclosure and consent. A business's own exemption may not cover the vendor.

### Gaps
- I could not verify SOC 2 or HIPAA status for Avoca, Netic, Sameday, Goodcall, Smith.ai, Hostie, Loman, Maple, Kea or Yelp, because trust pages were unreachable. No data-training-policy statements were found except Slang's de-identified-data clause.
