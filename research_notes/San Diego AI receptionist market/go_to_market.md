# Go-to-Market for a Solo Founder Selling an AI Phone Receptionist to San Diego Trades and Restaurants (as of 2026-10-13)

> Method note: page fetching was blocked by the session's egress policy (housecallpro.com, indiehackers.com, yespress.io all refused), so every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Most sources are vendor or agency blogs with a stake in the numbers. Each item is labelled **[data]** (a dataset or published figure, even if vendor-produced), **[vendor claim]**, or **[anecdote]** (one founder's or one post's account). Reddit and X threads did not show up in any search, so firsthand founder experience is thin.

## 1. Which acquisition channels work for selling to trades (supply houses, associations, BNI/chambers, Nextdoor/Facebook, LSA ecosystem, agency partners, cold calling/door-to-door, LinkedIn)?

### Takeaway
No source measured channel effectiveness for selling AI receptionists to trades. The best evidence points to warm and in-person channels: referrals convert roughly 10 times better than cold lists, contractors often prefer face-to-face contact, and a PHCC San Diego trade expo is coming up on Jan 30, 2027. It also points to piggybacking on the paid-lead ecosystem, since contractors paying $40-$100+ per Google LSA lead get no value from a lead they miss. Cold-call benchmarks are low (about 2.5% of dials become meetings), and LinkedIn has no evidence for this buyer at all.

### Cited Findings
**In-person and relationship channels**
- [data] About half of contractor customers in one distributor survey preferred only face-to-face interaction. Contractors mainly want to know whether you have the product, what it costs, and how fast they can get it — [Supply House Times](https://www.supplyht.com/articles/105674-considerations-when-selling-online-to-the-contractor-customer)
- [anecdote, 2015] A founder selling software to construction firms said they started at industry trade shows and industry-specific events, and pitched as high in the organization as possible — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9025498)
- [data] PHCC San Diego is a member organization of plumbing and HVACR contractors, technicians, manufacturers and suppliers in San Diego County (local, state and national tiers). Its one-day San Diego Trades Expo for plumbing, heating and cooling is scheduled for **January 30, 2027** — [PHCC San Diego](https://phccsd.org/); [Trades Expo 2027](https://phccsd.org/trades-expo/); [About](https://phccsd.org/about-8/)
- [data] UA Plumbers & Steamfitters Local 230 covers San Diego and Imperial Counties. These are union shops, mostly larger commercial contractors and less likely to buy an SMB receptionist — [UA Local 230](https://www.ualocal230.org/)
- [observation] Field crews resist software that feels like a burden that "only helps the office". Tools sell better when the field benefits too, for example the owner on the truck getting a text with the job — [Building Transformations](https://www.buildingtransformations.org/articles/selling-technology-to-building-contractors)

**Cold outbound benchmarks (proxies; no door-to-door data was found)**
- [data, vendor-compiled] Average B2B cold-call-to-meeting conversion is about 2.5% (1 meeting per 40 dials), and top teams reach 5-8% — [Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-calling-statistics)
- [data, vendor-compiled] Gong Labs: across 300M+ cold calls, the average connect rate was 5.4% and the top quartile 13.3% — [Leads at Scale](https://leadsatscale.com/insights/cold-calling-benchmarks-b2b-sales)
- [data, vendor-compiled] Cold lists convert 1.5-2% dial-to-meeting, while warm introductions and referrals convert **15-25%** — [Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/lead-conversion-rate-benchmarks) / [SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/glossary/cold-calling-conversion-rate) (per search summary)
- [data] Technology and SaaS dial-to-meeting rates often fall below 1%, while business services sit around 2.4-2.6% — [Precision Outbound](https://precisionoutbound.xyz/posts/cold-calling-conversion-rate-benchmarks-for-b2b-saas)

**The Google Local Services Ads (LSA) ecosystem**
- [data, agency-compiled] SearchLight Digital's Feb 2026 dataset of 888 contractors puts average LSA cost per lead at about **$39 for electrical, $51 for HVAC and $57 for plumbing**. Major metros run $45-$80 — [AdGenius](https://adgenius.com/blog/google-local-services-ads-cost-per-lead/); [Skill Mammoth](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/local-service-ads-cost)
- [data, agency-compiled] Emergency AC calls in peak summer can push CPL **above $100** in major metros. The blended dataset showed a 43.9% booking rate, $233 cost per paying customer and 7.84x return on ad spend — [ClickTrack Marketing](https://www.clicktrackmarketing.com/blog/local-services-ads-cost); [BookedFriday](https://bookedfriday.com/local-services-ads-cost)
- [agency claim] "A missed LSA lead almost never calls back." Actual CPL depends on response speed and booking rate — [Ryn Digital](https://ryndigital.com/local-services-ads-statistics-2026/); [Valley Marketing Group](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/google-local-services-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/)

**Agency, web-designer and reseller partnerships**
- [vendor claim] My AI Front Desk's white-label program sells receptionists to agencies at **$54.99 wholesale each**, and the agency sets the client price. "Many successful resellers charge $250-$500/month." Its best-fit partners are marketing agencies, MSPs, web studios and vertical specialists — [My AI Front Desk reseller page](https://llms.myaifrontdesk.com/white-label-reseller-agencies); [reseller profits blog](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/blogs/boosting-your-business-ai-receptionist-reseller-profits-explained)
- [anecdote, Indie Hackers] One post recommends selling through existing agencies rather than direct, testing local services first (dental, legal, home services), and pitching "conversion and lead capture" rather than "voice AI" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-m-testing-agency-distribution-for-a-website-voice-agent-product-221376fa66)
- [anecdote, Indie Hackers] Another post claims that the builders with real traction got it through partnerships with home-service software (Jobber, ServiceTitan) or strong local reputations in specific markets — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)
- [data, YC launch] Acrely, a YC voice-AI startup for home services, was building scheduling for real estate agents when "home services businesses came inbound to us asking to use it". It pivoted and landed **6 customers in 2 weeks** — [YC Launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OH3-acrely-the-ai-customer-service-representative-for-home-services)

**Agency marketing to trades (illustrates competitor tactics)**
- [vendor claim, unverified] GoHighLevel-hosted agency funnels pitch "Stop losing five-figure leads to your voicemail" and claim an HVAC contractor doubled revenue to $60k/month and a plumber tripled leads. These are typical, unverifiable agency claims — [GHL page](https://app.gohighlevel.com/v2/preview/mNgoCYMrw8iDpfGBJZbf)
- [vendor claim] Local AI agencies (e.g. Astucia in Chicago) bundle the AI receptionist with chatbots, review automation and websites for HVAC, plumbing, roofing and cleaning — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/astucia)

### Inferences
- Warm and referral channels (BNI, chamber, PHCC, supply-house relationships, customer referrals) should get most of a solo founder's time. The 15-25% vs 1.5-2% gap means one warm intro is worth roughly 10 cold dials, and contractors skew toward face-to-face.
- The PHCC San Diego Trades Expo (Jan 30, 2027) falls about 15 weeks out. It is the single best-timed local event to exhibit at or walk with a live demo number. Contacting the chapter about vendor or sponsor slots now is a reasonable first move.
- Supply-house counters (Ferguson, Winsupply, Johnstone) are a plausible place to catch owner-operators between 6 and 8am, but **no source documents software vendors selling successfully at counter days**. Treat it as an experiment: ask the branch manager about a "contractor breakfast" or counter-day slot.
- LSA-running contractors are the highest-intent segment: they already pay $40-$100+ per lead, and an unanswered lead is wasted money. A good pitch is "you're paying ~$57 per plumbing lead; every one you miss is $57 burned plus the job." Local PPC/LSA agencies and web designers serving trades are natural referral partners (they look better when leads convert). A per-seat wholesale or referral fee mirrors the existing reseller market.
- Integrating with Jobber or Housecall Pro is a differentiator against ServiceTitan's native agent. ServiceTitan customers are mostly larger shops (see Q3).

### Gaps
- No primary data or founder posts on conversion from supply-house counter days, BNI, chambers, Nextdoor or Facebook groups for AI receptionist sales. Reddit and X threads did not surface in any search.
- No San Diego-specific LSA CPL data. No IEC, ACCA, San Diego BNI or chamber listings surfaced; they need direct lookup (e.g. IEC San Diego, ACCA, San Diego Regional Chamber, BNI San Diego region).
- No evidence either way on LinkedIn for owner-operator trades.
- No door-to-door or walk-in conversion benchmark for local B2B SaaS was found.

## 2. Restaurants: channels (POS partners, associations, delivery-era pain points), and whether restaurants pay for AI phone answering (Loman, Slang.ai, Hostie traction)

### Takeaway
Restaurants clearly do pay. Three venture-backed vendors (Slang.ai, Hostie, Loman) have raised money on it at **$199-$399+/month entry prices**. But the category is crowded, and Square now offers native AI voice ordering. Vendor datasets say restaurants miss about 23-36% of peak-hour calls. For a solo founder, restaurants look like a harder secondary segment than trades.

### Cited Findings
- [data, press] Hostie announced a **$12M Series A led by Obvious Ventures in July 2026, bringing total funding to $16M**. It says it handles "millions of guest conversations" for reservations, orders and event leads — [YesPress, Aug 2026](https://yespress.io/hostie)
- [vendor comparison] Hostie's entry plan starts at **$199/month**, and Slang.ai's at **$399/month** — [Slang.ai vs Hostie](https://www.slang.ai/blog-slang-ai-vs-hostie-complete-2026-comparison-guide)
- [conflicting data] Slang.ai is reported to have raised $68M in total (including a $36M Series B) per a 2026 comparison site, while Slang's own blog references a "$20M in funding" announcement. It self-reports 2,000+ restaurant locations — [Slang.ai blog](https://www.slang.ai/blog); [Runable](https://runable.com/blogs/best-ai-for-restaurants) (per search summary)
- [data] Loman (Austin) raised a **$3.5M seed in Aug 2025** (CB Insights lists $3.7M total) from Antler, TenOneTen and Next Coast. Named customers include Dechecho's Pizzeria, WingSnob and Nick's Place — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/lomanai); [CloudTalk review](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/loman-ai-review/)
- [vendor data] Hostie's 500k-call study: human-only peak answer rate was **64%** vs **97%** with AI, and lunch-rush missed calls fell from 23% to 3% — [Hostie study](https://www.hostie.ai/resources/hostie-ai-500k-call-study-missed-calls-reduction); [lunch-rush case](https://hostie.ai/resources/lunch-rush-case-study-cutting-missed-calls-23-to-3-percent-hostie-ai-500k-dataset)
- [vendor data] Maple's analysis of 1.2M+ calls found restaurants miss **33%** of incoming calls during peak service — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/blog/the-state-of-restaurant-phone-communication-insights-from-1-million-calls)
- [data] **Square has native AI voice ordering**. It works only on Square-provided business numbers; existing lines can forward order calls to one. Confirmed orders go to the kitchen or POS — [Square Help](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8568-take-orders-with-ai-powered-voice-ordering); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/square-product-update-voice-ordering-ai-assistant/802331/)
- [vendor claim] Toast has no built-in AI phone answering as of 2026, and third parties connect to its POS. SoundHound announced a Toast integration in 2023, and VOICEplug, Certus and ActiveMenus all claim Toast support — [VOICEplug](https://voiceplug.ai/how-toast-ai-phone-answering-works-for-restaurants/); [BusinessWire 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230110005358/en)
- [vendor claim] VOICEplug reports a 94.5% phone-order conversion rate at a 19-location pizza chain on Toast — [VOICEplug](https://voiceplug.ai/phone-systems-that-integrate-with-toast-and-square/)

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay is proven by funded competitors, but those competitors have reservation and POS integrations, sales teams and brand. A solo founder competing head-on in full-service dining is at a disadvantage.
- A narrower restaurant wedge is more defensible: independent, family-owned, Spanish/English, phone-heavy takeout or catering spots on Toast or Clover (not Square, which now has native voice ordering), where the job is answering hours, menu and catering questions, taking a callback or order details, and texting the owner. Local, in-person setup is a differentiator against national self-serve vendors.
- Restaurant phone pain peaks during the rush, which is also when a walk-in pitch is unwelcome. Visit at 2-4pm.

### Gaps
- No Toast Partner Connect or Square App Marketplace listing data for AI answering; I could not confirm what is officially listed.
- No information surfaced on the San Diego chapter of the California Restaurant Association or local restaurant groups.
- No independent (non-vendor) survey of AI-answering adoption or churn in restaurants. Hostie's customer count was not found.

## 3. Pricing models: flat monthly vs per-minute vs per-call vs per-booked-job; acceptable price points; setup fees; trials; contract terms; evidence on conversion and churn

### Takeaway
Self-serve AI receptionist prices in 2026 cluster at **$39-$149/month** (Ring-Ready $39, Rosie $49-$299, Goodcall $59-$79). Human or hybrid services cost $235-$1,700+/month, and agency done-for-you offers $250-$800/month plus $800-$2K setup. SMB SaaS churns about 3-5%+ a month. No source directly compared flat vs per-minute vs per-booked-job conversion for trades.

### Cited Findings
**Price points (2026)**
- [vendor pricing via comparison sites] **Rosie**: $49/mo for 250 min, $149 for 1,000, $299 for 2,000. **Goodcall**: $79 entry (one source says $59) for 100 unique callers with unlimited minutes, $0.50 per extra caller, 15% annual discount — [Commerce Pundit](https://www.commercepundit.com/blog/ai-receptionist-cost/); [Aira](https://www.getaira.io/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing-guide)
- [vendor pricing] **Ruby** (human, per minute): about $250 for 50 min ($5/min), $395 for 100, $720 for 200, $1,725 for 500 (some sources list $235-$319 entry). **Smith.ai** (per call): human plans about $292.50-$300 for 30 calls plus $11.50 per extra call, AI-only about $95-$97.50 for 30 calls — [Voksha](https://voksha.com/guide/ai-receptionist-pricing-guide/); [LimePhone](https://limephone.io/post/best-ai-receptionists)
- [comparison-site estimate] At 200 calls/month, Smith.ai could cost about $1,965 vs about $149 for Rosie. Overage and tier thresholds drive real bills — [Voksha](https://voksha.com/guide/best-ai-receptionists-2026/)
- [vendor pricing] Ring-Ready markets to plumbers and HVAC at **$39/mo** (only 2 Trustpilot reviews) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ring-ready.com)
- [vendor pricing] Restaurant vertical: Hostie from $199/mo, Slang from $399/mo — [Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/blog-slang-ai-vs-hostie-complete-2026-comparison-guide)
- [anecdote/projection, Indie Hackers] Proposed agency model: **$800-$2K setup plus $300-$800/month per client** for local service businesses, with "2-3 weeks to first revenue" (author projection, not results) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)
- [vendor claim] White-label resellers commonly charge $250-$500/mo on top of $54.99 wholesale — [My AI Front Desk](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/blogs/boosting-your-business-ai-receptionist-reseller-profits-explained)
- [product] A one-time $1,497 plumbing booking bot also exists, i.e. one-time-license models exist — [Gumroad](https://justinvincent5.gumroad.com/l/biziwu)

**Churn and retention**
- [data, compiled] SMB B2B SaaS monthly churn is **3-5%** (Optifai, 939 companies), versus 1-2% for enterprise — [Optifai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/b2b-saas-churn-rate-benchmark/)
- [data, compiled] ChartMogul median monthly customer churn is **6.5% below $300k ARR**. Another 2026 benchmark puts SMB at 4.5% a month (42.3% a year) — [RetentionCheck](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/smb-saas)
- [math] 3% monthly churn means 30.6% annual churn; 5% monthly means about 46% — [Koji](https://www.koji.so/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- [vendor opinion] AI receptionist agency churn is driven by reporting activity (call counts) instead of outcomes. "Local business owners don't think in calls — they think in jobs booked." Capture a pre-onboarding baseline so ROI can be shown — [MyVoiceAIConnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/how-to-show-roi-to-ai-receptionist-clients)

**Competitive pricing context: platform-native agents**
- [data] ServiceTitan's AI Voice Agent is fully native: it books against live capacity and reschedules. It is sold standalone or in Contact Center Pro, and one early adopter booked 500+ jobs in 30 days (23% of its monthly jobs) — [ServiceTitan blog](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-ai-voice-agents-call-booking); [ServiceTitan VA page](https://plg.servicetitan.com/va)
- [data] Jobber announced "Jobber Teammate" (an internal AI agent, in beta) on **Sept 23, 2026**. Jobber Voice is for staff, not callers; no Jobber-native customer-facing receptionist was found — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jobber-unveils-jobber-teammate-the-first-ai-agent-for-home-and-commercial-service-businesses-to-plan-act-and-learn-like-a-real-team-member-302886414.html); [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/jn-2026-qa/)

### Inferences
- The price anchor in a trade owner's head will be $49-$99 self-serve tools, against $250+ for human answering services. A done-for-you local offer at about **$149-$299/month flat**, with a generous included-call allowance, sits between "cheap DIY bot" and "human answering service." Small shops can afford it against one saved job (HVAC replacements are often quoted around $12k by vendors).
- Flat monthly with a call cap is simpler for trades than per-minute pricing. Per-minute bills spike in exactly the busy season when the product is most useful, and comparison sites already warn buyers about overage. Per-booked-job pricing aligns incentives but needs attribution and disputes are likely; consider it only as a pilot "guarantee" (see Q5), not the core model.
- A setup fee ($0-$500) is common among agencies. For first customers, waive it in exchange for a case study. Month-to-month with an annual discount (Goodcall-style 15%) is the market norm.
- With SMB churn of 3-6.5% a month, retention depends on outcome reporting: a weekly or monthly "jobs captured, after-hours calls, estimated revenue" text to the owner.
- ServiceTitan shops are already pitched a native agent. Target Jobber, Housecall Pro and "pen and paper" shops, where no native caller-facing agent exists.

### Gaps
- No primary study compared flat, per-minute and per-booked-job conversion or churn for trades.
- No data on free-trial conversion rates for SMB SaaS or AI receptionists specifically, or on month-to-month vs annual churn.
- ServiceTitan Voice Agent pricing was not found.

## 4. Positioning angles that resonate with trades (missed-call ROI math, after-hours/emergency, bilingual Spanish, local support, no contract, owner text; proof via missed-call audit)

### Takeaway
The missed-call ROI story is the dominant pitch in the market. Its statistics are widely repeated but poorly sourced: missed-call rates run from 27% to 62%, and the "$1,200 per missed call" figure has no visible method. A founder should prove ROI with the prospect's own numbers (a missed-call audit or call-tracking test) rather than cite industry stats. San Diego's high share of Spanish-speaking households in South County and Escondido supports a bilingual angle.

### Cited Findings
- [vendor-reported, attributed to Invoca] Home services businesses miss about **27%** of inbound calls — [CallJolt](https://calljolt.com/blog/guides/home-service-business-missed-call-statistics); [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/missed-calls/)
- [vendor-reported, unlinked] A "ServiceTitan analysis of 50,000 contractor lines" found **62%** of calls unanswered. It is repeated widely, but no blog links the underlying report — [Hashlogics](https://www.hashlogics.com/blog/the-62-percent-problem)
- [vendor-reported, attributed to Invoca 2026] Overall answer rate is 52% (73% excluding calls under 30s). Answer rates by trade: plumbing 74%, HVAC 34%, construction 32%. Invoca's customer base skews toward larger advertisers — [AINORA](https://ainora.lt/blog/hvac-service-call-statistics-2026); [Skygnosis](https://skygnosis.com/insights/the-calls-you-never-knew-you-missed)
- [unverified] "Average $1,200 lost per missed call" is repeated widely, with no methodology found — [Instant Business Pro](https://instantbusinesspro.ai/post/cost-of-missed-calls); [VoIP-Int](https://voip-int.com/blog/voip-international-blog-posts-4/hvac-contractors-lose-45k-120k-a-year-to-missed-calls-here-s-the-real-data-273)
- [note] One article removed an earlier "Invoca" figure as unverifiable, which shows how unreliable the stat ecosystem is — per search summary of [Skygnosis](https://skygnosis.com/insights/the-calls-you-never-knew-you-missed)
- [vendor claim] "Callers who reach no one don't leave a voicemail" — [800.com via BusinessWire, Mar 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260312119236/en)
- [vendor framing] The average HVAC replacement is about $12,000, so one missed call a month outweighs the software cost — per search summary of agency/vendor pages ([GHL page](https://app.gohighlevel.com/v2/preview/gy5Aebd8YAZv8dAJVDzN))
- [data] San Diego household language (ACS 2024 via DataUSA, primary shared household language Spanish): **57.6%** in Chula Vista West and National City; **58.3%** in South San Diego/Otay Mesa; **37.4%** in Escondido East; 31.4% in Mid-City; 26.7% in Lemon Grove/Spring Valley; only 8.9% in the coastal SW city area — [DataUSA Chula Vista/NC](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/san-diego-county-southwest-chula-vista-west-national-city-cities-puma-ca); [South/Otay](https://la.datausa.io/profile/geo/san-diego-county-south-san-diego-city-southotay-mesa-south-bay-puma-ca); [Escondido](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/san-diego-county-northwest-escondido-city-east-puma-ca); [Mid-City](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/79500US0607317); [Coastal](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/san-diego-county-west-san-diego-city-southwestcentral-coastal-puma-ca)
- [vendor opinion] Report "jobs booked," not "calls handled", and capture a baseline before onboarding — [MyVoiceAIConnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/how-to-show-roi-to-ai-receptionist-clients)
- [agency claim] An LSA lead that goes unanswered "almost never calls back" — [Ryn Digital](https://ryndigital.com/local-services-ads-statistics-2026/)

### Inferences
- Positioning stack, best-supported first:
  1. "Every missed call is a job your competitor gets": prove it with **their** data (a 2-week call log or call-tracking forward, or a review of phone-carrier missed-call reports) instead of the 27%/62%/$1,200 stats, which a skeptical owner can easily dismiss.
  2. "You're paying $50-$100 per LSA lead; don't let voicemail burn it": aimed at LSA advertisers.
  3. After-hours and emergency capture: HVAC in heat waves, burst-pipe plumbing.
  4. Bilingual English/Spanish, which is credible and differentiated in South Bay, National City, Chula Vista, Escondido and Mid-City where Spanish is the main household language in 30-58% of homes.
  5. "Local San Diego person who sets it up and answers my phone," no contract, and an owner text with name, address, problem and urgency.
- The owner-text-with-the-job angle matches the "think in jobs, not calls" finding and the field-vs-office adoption barrier.
- Against ServiceTitan's native agent: target smaller shops not on ServiceTitan. Against cheap self-serve ($39-$79): sell done-for-you setup, local accountability and outcome reports.

### Gaps
- No primary Invoca or ServiceTitan report could be accessed to verify the headline missed-call statistics.
- No source tested which message resonates most with trades (no A/B or survey data).
- No countywide Hispanic share or count of trade businesses and restaurants was found (Census QuickFacts and County Business Patterns NAICS 238/722 would be the sources).

## 5. Offer design for first customers (pilot/case-study pricing, guarantees, done-for-you setup, referral incentives)

### Takeaway
No source documented a tested first-customer offer for this category. The available evidence (warm-referral conversion, outcome-based retention, agency setup-fee norms, the reseller wholesale model) supports a done-for-you pilot with a baseline audit, outcome reporting and a referral loop. That is an inferred design, not proven.

### Cited Findings
- [anecdote/projection] Agency model of $800-$2K setup plus $300-$800/mo; local services as the initial target — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)
- [data, compiled] Referrals and warm intros convert 15-25% vs 1.5-2% for cold lists — [Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/lead-conversion-rate-benchmarks)
- [vendor opinion] Capture baseline data before onboarding, or clients have no reference point for later reports — [MyVoiceAIConnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/how-to-show-roi-to-ai-receptionist-clients)
- [data, YC] Acrely reached 6 home-service customers in 2 weeks off inbound demand, which suggests trades actively want this — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OH3-acrely-the-ai-customer-service-representative-for-home-services)
- [vendor pricing] Self-serve competitors at $39-$79/mo set the floor; Goodcall offers 15% off annual — [Commerce Pundit](https://www.commercepundit.com/blog/ai-receptionist-cost/)

### Inferences (proposed offer; not evidence-tested)
- **Free missed-call audit** (1-2 weeks): forward overflow and after-hours calls, or review carrier logs, then report "you missed X calls, Y after hours, ~Z likely jobs." This creates the baseline that retention depends on.
- **Founding-customer pilot**: first 5-10 customers get setup free (vs a $300-$500 list setup fee), 30 days free or $99 for the first month, then a locked rate (e.g. $149/mo) in exchange for a testimonial, a case study with numbers, and 2 referrals introduced.
- **Guarantee**: "If it doesn't capture at least one job you'd have missed in the first 30 days, you don't pay," which is easy to honor at small scale and directly addresses ROI doubt.
- **Done-for-you**: in-person setup at the shop, forwarding configured, a script in the owner's words, Spanish on, a test call together.
- **Referral incentive**: a free month for both sides per referred customer. Also offer a referral fee or wholesale margin to local LSA/PPC agencies and web designers, mirroring the $250-$500 resale-over-$55-wholesale market.

## 6. A realistic first-90-days plan for a solo founder with one pilot customer (activity targets and conversion benchmarks)
